# Buyer Demand Evidence for Used and Refurbished Lab Equipment

Research date: 2026-10-14. Several primary sites could not be reached from this environment (DNS failures for labmanager.com, cen.acs.org, instrumentbusinessoutlook.com, wisconsin.edu, seedinglabs.org). Findings from those sites come from search-result snippets and summaries and could not be checked against the full pages. They are marked "(snippet only)".

## 1. Market size and growth (global and US)

### Takeaway
Paid market reports put "refurbished laboratory equipment" at roughly $25-28B globally (2024-2026), growing 4-7% a year, with the US at about $7B. An independent industry analyst (SDI) instead puts used equipment at only about 2-3% of lab equipment sales, about $2B a year. That is a 10x gap, so the report figures should be treated as unreliable, broadly defined numbers.

### Cited Findings
- The Business Research Company (Feb 2026) gives $26.25B (2025), $28.17B (2026) and $36.96B by 2030, at a 7.3% CAGR. A March 2026 press release from the same firm gives about $39B by 2030, with North America the largest region at about $13B. The firm's own figures are not consistent with each other. — [ResearchAndMarkets listing](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6190898/refurbished-laboratory-equipment-market-report); [press release, Norfolk Daily News](https://norfolkdailynews.com/online_features/press_releases/refurbished-laboratory-equipment-market-2026-improving-research-accessibility/article_ebf20c4c-bd1d-5cc7-a727-256ffdeb625b.html)
- A Global Industry Analysts-style report (via GII Korea) gives $25.6B (2024) rising to $33.0B by 2030, a 4.3% CAGR. It puts the US at $7.0B in 2024. — [GII Korea](https://m.giikorea.co.kr/report/go1737386-refurbished-laboratory-equipment.html)
- A LP Information/Sharefair report gives about $24.5B (2022) rising to $35.1B (2029), a 5.3% CAGR. — [Sharefair](https://markets.sharefair.com/en/detail/LP0913515475BNM-Global-Refurbished-Laboratory-Equipment-Market-Growth-Status-and-Outlook-2023-2029)
- Contrary estimate: Michael Tice of Strategic Directions International (SDI, a lab-instrument market analyst) puts used equipment at about 2-3% of global lab equipment sales, about $2B a year (C&EN, 2024, vol. 102 issue 18; snippet only). — [C&EN, "What happens to old scientific instruments?"](https://cen.acs.org/business/instrumentation/happens-old-scientific-instruments/102/i18)
- Lab Manager (circa 2025, snippet only) reports that inquiries for used lab equipment rose 23%, compared with +9% for refurbished and +22% for new. The source and period of these figures were not visible. The article links the rise to tariffs and tight budgets. — [Lab Manager](https://www.labmanager.com/how-tariffs-and-tight-budgets-are-driving-demand-for-refurbished-lab-equipment-34007)

### Inferences
- The $25B+ report figures probably fold in refurbished medical and diagnostic devices, servicing, and new-equipment-adjacent categories, or they are simply extrapolated. Pay-to-read "market reports" in this category are low-credibility SEO products: their figures disagree with each other and none publishes its methods. SDI's roughly $2B figure for true secondhand instrument sales is a more defensible anchor. Even 1% of the US share of that is a large addressable pool for a small reseller.
- The supply side is clearly active in 2026. EquipNet's October 2026 calendar lists closure auctions for Novartis, Bayer and Evotec, and Heritage Global and Rabin list biotech wind-down sales (Cytokine Therapy ABC, Biomea Fusion, Bolt Projects). — [EquipNet auctions](https://www.equipnet.com/auctions/); [Heritage Global](https://www.hgpauction.com/auction-category/biotech-pharmaceutical-medical/); [Rabin](https://rabin.com/industries/pharma-biotech/). Abundant supply implies competitive pricing and pressure on margins.

### Gaps
- I could not access any market report's methodology. No figure specific to Massachusetts or New England was found.
- I found no independent data on transaction volume (for example, eBay lab-equipment GMV or LabX listing counts).

## 2. Who the buyers are

### Takeaway
There is documented demand from startups, labs in low- and middle-income countries, and teaching settings. Most of this evidence comes from vendors or nonprofits rather than buyer surveys. I found no survey that measures what share of academic labs buy used equipment.

### Cited Findings
- **Biotech startups:** Lab Manager (2018) quotes a dealer CEO saying a pre-owned high-end mass spec can cost as little as 10% of new. BioSurplus offers extra discounts to start-up labs, and its executive says the company itself began as a start-up lab that bought pre-owned equipment. The same CEO warns that equipment from liquidators can cost more to repair than it saved. — [Lab Manager 2018](https://www.labmanager.com/used-equipment-a-boon-to-start-up-labs-2266)
- **Startups lease as an alternative:** Excedr, a leasing company and so an interested party, says buying instruments outright can mean "six- or even seven-figure checks" before the first experiment and can consume a large share of a seed round. — [Excedr](https://www.excedr.com/blog/why-biotech-startups-lease-instead-of-buy)
- Dealers run dedicated startup programs, which signals that vendors see startups as a target segment. — [New Life Scientific startup page](https://newlifescientific.com/pages/startup)
- **Labs in LMICs (Seeding Labs, Boston-based):** In the 2015 cycle, 67 university departments applied and 16 were selected. In 2016 the organization said demand was "far outstripping" capacity, with equipment for only about a quarter of applicants. The average shipment is worth about $100K at used-market value. Recipients pay a fee scaled to their country's income level that covers warehousing and ocean freight, so they are paying buyers, not just recipients. Eligibility is limited to degree-granting universities in LMICs. The 2023 cycle had deadlines on 18 Aug and 13 Oct. — [UGA Peanut Lab](https://site.caes.uga.edu/ftfpeanutlab/?p=1768); [CST blog](https://blog.cellsignal.com/sowing-seeds-of-research-success-in-developing-countries); [TerraViva Grants](https://www.terravivagrants.org/?p=17993); [TerraViva eligibility](https://www.terravivagrants.org/?p=104664); [2024 application form](https://seedinglabs.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Instrumental-Access-Application-Form-downloadable.pdf)
- **High schools:** Caltech's LEAP project collects donated funds to buy the equipment that public high school teachers request, such as balances, glassware, microscopes and PPE. Its spring 2025 drive served two La Puente, CA high schools. — [Caltech LEAP impact](https://leap.caltech.edu/impact)
- **Massachusetts teaching labs:** The Massachusetts Life Sciences Center has funded equipment purchases rather than donations. Examples are grants of up to $250K to vocational schools, community colleges and workforce programs (program launched 2010), $93,410 to Greater Lawrence Technical HS in 2017, and $200K to Bunker Hill CC in 2013. — [BioSpace on MLSC](https://www.biospace.com/b-massachusetts-life-sciences-center-b-launches-equipment-matching-grant-program-for-skills-training); [WHAV 2017](https://whav.net/2017/04/11/whittier-vo-tech-receives-100000-for-life-science-training/); [BHCC 2013](https://www.bhcc.edu/news/2013/april/bhcc-receives-200000-to-expand-biotechnology-program.html)
- **Academic R&D labs on tight budgets:** In a 2013 thread on MIT's labnetwork list (cleanroom and facilities managers), a dealer pitches used equipment. In a 2020 thread, members discuss where replacement-equipment money comes from (the PI's grant or a university budget). — [labnetwork 2013](https://mtl.mit.edu/pipermail/labnetwork/2013-March/000805.html); [labnetwork 2020](https://mtl.mit.edu/pipermail/labnetwork/2020-October/004413.html)

### Inferences
- Seeding Labs' roughly 4:1 ratio of applicants to awards (2015-16 data, now dated) is the strongest hard evidence of unmet demand, but it measures demand at heavily subsidized prices.
- Teaching labs in MA appear to be funded mainly through MLSC grants for buying equipment. A seller of tested, documented equipment could sell into those grant-funded purchases.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence about MassBioEd's equipment programs. Searches returned only Caltech LEAP.
- I found nothing usable on DIY-bio or community labs (Genspace, BosLab) or on buying by CROs.
- I found no buyer survey (for example, from Lab Manager or SDI) giving the share of labs that buy used equipment.
- I found no current (2025-26) Seeding Labs waitlist data, because the site could not be reached.

## 3. Funding pressure in 2025-2026 and the shift to used equipment

### Takeaway
Funding pressure is real and well documented: 2,291 grants were terminated in Feb-Aug 2025, capital budgets were cut, and disbursements were slow in 2026. However, I found no independent news or survey that shows labs moving to used equipment as a result. Only vendors make that claim.

### Cited Findings
- A peer-reviewed study (PMC) finds that NIH terminated 2,291 active grants between Feb and Aug 2025, rescinding $2.45B of a $5.08B investment. — [PMC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13037894/)
- CRS reports that, as of April 10, 2026, HHS had terminated 1,392 NIH awards with $539M in unliquidated obligations. — [CRS IF13131](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13131)
- FY2026 NIH funding was enacted at about $47.5B program level (+1.0%) through P.L. 119-74/75, which rejected the proposed 40% cut to $27.9B. — [CRS R43341](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R43341). CASRAI, a lesser-known source, says the bill was signed on Feb 3, 2026 and reports disbursements of only about $6B by March 2026 against a typical $9B. — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/nih-fy2026-appropriations-enacted-what-it-means-for-grantees)
- The 15% indirect-cost cap (Feb 2025) set off lawsuits and was later blocked by a district court. — [Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/nih-slashes-overhead-payments-research-sparking-outrage); [Health Policy Watch](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/nih-limits-overhead-cost-funding-to-15-cutting-off-9-billion-to-biomedical-research-institutions/)
- An Instrument Business Outlook survey (June 2025, snippet only, sample size unknown) found that 71% of labs reported operating budget cuts of more than 10%, 71% reported similar capital budget cuts, and 70% had reduced spending on devices under $50K. — [IBO](https://instrumentbusinessoutlook.com/nih-disruptions-and-lab-tool-companies-stocks-react/)
- Capstone says NIH's shift to multi-year up-front funding reduces the number of awards and delays instrument purchases. — [Capstone DC](https://capstonedc.com/insights/healthcare-insights/untangling-the-outlook-for-nih-funding-and-its-impact-on-pharma/)
- NPR (Feb 2025) describes labs bracing for cuts, including a researcher working on roughly 20-year-old equipment. — [NPR via Houston Public Media](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/npr/2025/02/24/nx-s1-5307157/medical-research-labs-brace-for-possible-funding-cuts-that-could-disrupt-their-work/)
- Hamilton Company created a Research Support Grant of up to $150K, twice a year, for labs hit by funding cuts. This is evidence that vendors are responding to budget stress. — [Hamilton](https://www.hamiltoncompany.com/hamilton-research-support-grant)
- Vendor content links refurbished equipment to tariffs and NIH cuts, for example LQA (May 2025) citing 3-6 month lead times on new equipment, and a "Lab Budget Survival Guide 2026". This is marketing, not evidence. — [LQA](https://www.lqa.com/resources/navigating-evolving-tariff-policies-and-supply-chain-uncertainty-with-refurbished-lab-equipment/); [Innovative BioSci](https://www.innovativebiosci.com/blogs/news/lab-budget-survival-guide-2026-how-to-keep-research-running-through-nih-cuts-and-tariffs)

### Inferences
- Shrinking capital budgets and cuts to sub-$50K device spending point two ways. Labs may delay purchases entirely, which shrinks demand. Or they may switch to cheaper secondhand units, which grows demand for used equipment. The data do not show which effect dominates.
- At the same time, closures add supply, as auction calendars show.

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads (r/labrats, r/PhD) were returned by search. The tool did not index them, so I have no first-hand buyer accounts from 2025-26.
- No STAT, Science, Nature or Boston Globe article tying cuts to used-equipment buying was found.

## 4. How academic labs buy used equipment (procurement rules)

### Takeaway
Most universities allow used equipment but put friction on eBay and individual sellers. Common hurdles are that card purchases are capped (often at $5K for capital items), eBay is discouraged or needs pre-approval, and services from individuals cannot be paid by card. Selling as a registered business that accepts purchase orders and provides an invoice, W-9 and written warranty removes most of this friction.

### Cited Findings
- **Virginia Tech** "highly discourages" eBay. It cites no recourse for damaged or dead items, says eBay does not accept purchase orders, and says PO protections are lost on card purchases. It recommends asking the seller to sell directly to the university, and it requires a separate eBay account with no stored card. Its used-equipment procedure encourages buying used when practical but excludes pass-through auction sites such as eBay. — [VT eBay guidance](https://www.procurement.vt.edu/content/dam/procurement_vt_edu/procedures/how/eBay.pdf); [VT auction procedures](https://procurement.vt.edu/content/dam/procurement_vt_edu/procedures/how/auction%20_procedures.pdf); [VT eBay purchases](https://www.procurement.vt.edu/content/dam/procurement_vt_edu/how/Ebay-Purchases.pdf)
- **UW System** allows eBay on the P-card if the risk of loss is minimized. For operating equipment it expects independent verification that the item works, and it encourages checking with purchasing first (snippet only). — [UW SYS 351 App. B](https://www.wisconsin.edu/uw-policies/uw-system-administrative-policies/purchasing-card-compliance-policy/sys-351-appendix-b-commonly-questioned-items/)
- **Purdue** requires contacting Procurement before buying used equipment on eBay. — [Purdue FAQ](https://apps.it.purdue.edu/sites/BusinessOffice/Faq/Item/1010)
- **Arizona** permits eBay and PayPal on the P-card within its guidelines. — [UA neuroscience purchasing](https://neurosci.arizona.edu/purchasing)
- **LSU** allows used equipment only with the department head's justification, Procurement approval and a seller quote. — [LSU used equipment](https://lsu.edu/administration/ofa/procurement/a-z-policies-procedures/usedequipment.php)
- **Harvard:** the PCard cannot be used for capitalized equipment of $5,000 or more, or for services from individuals or single-employee businesses. PayPal purchases of goods must name the ultimate seller, and splitting a purchase to stay under the limit is prohibited. A Harvard Forest guide cites a $1,000 PCard limit, but that applies only to that unit. — [Harvard PCard at a glance](https://policies.fad.harvard.edu/resource/pcardataglancepdf); [Harvard prohibited purchases appendix](https://policies.fad.harvard.edu/resource/appendixaallowablerestrictedandprohibitedpurchasespdf); [Harvard Forest guidelines (2024)](https://harvardforest.fas.harvard.edu/sites/harvardforest.fas.harvard.edu/files/0_Harvard%20Forest%20Purchasing%20Guidelines_wAppendices_09.12.24.pdf)
- **Boston University:** competitive bidding is required above $25K, with bid documentation attached to the requisition. Below $25K, the department decides whether a price is fair and reasonable. Extra rules apply above $100K. The source is a 2022 BU Engineering PDF. — [BU ME Competitive Bidding](https://www.bu.edu/eng/files/2022/10/ME_Competitive-Bidding-.pdf)
- **Federal grant rules:** the micro-purchase threshold rose to $15K and the simplified acquisition threshold to $350K on Oct 1, 2025 (FAR adjustment). Institutions may self-certify a micro-purchase threshold of up to $50K under 2 CFR 200.320. Used equipment charged to federal awards follows the same 2 CFR 200.1 equipment definition and capitalization rules as new equipment. These come from secondary guides, so verify them against FAR 2.101. — [CASRAI thresholds](https://www.casrai.org/guides/simplified-acquisition-threshold-and-micro-purchase-threshold); [CASRAI used vs refurbished](https://casrai.org/guides/used-vs-refurbished-lab-equipment)

### Inferences
- The best fit is items under $5K that can go on a P-card from a business seller with an invoice and returns, and items under $15-25K that a department can buy without bids. Above about $25K (BU) a buyer needs quotes, so a seller should be ready to provide a formal quote and sole-source or "used opportunity" justification language.
- Selling as an individual eBay seller limits the academic market. University supplier registration (W-9, invoice, PO acceptance) is likely a prerequisite for repeat institutional sales.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the full policies for BU, MIT or UMass on used equipment and supplier onboarding, or BU's P-card limits.
- I found no explicit university statement on buying from individual (non-business) sellers of goods.

## 5. Price sensitivity, warranty, testing and returns

### Takeaway
Discounts of 30-70% off new are typical (vendor claims), and up to 90% for high-end instruments. The repeated buyer concern is non-working equipment without recourse, so testing, certification and returns are what turn "used" into something a lab can buy.

### Cited Findings
- Vendors commonly claim 30-70% savings off new (vendor and blog sources, so marketing). — [CASRAI guide](https://casrai.org/guides/used-vs-refurbished-lab-equipment); [New Life Scientific buyer's guide](https://newlifescientific.com/blogs/new-life-scientific-blog/used-lab-equipment-buyers-guide)
- A pre-owned high-end mass spec can cost as little as 10% of new (Lab Manager, 2018). — [Lab Manager](https://www.labmanager.com/used-equipment-a-boon-to-start-up-labs-2266)
- A 2007 GCI Tech Note describes buying two used centrifuges on eBay for under $1,000 each, about 3% of the new price. It advises watching comparable auctions before bidding. — [GCI Tech Notes](https://gcisolutions.com/library/gci_tech_notes/gcitn200704.html)
- C&EN (2024, snippet only) describes the refurbished channel, where units come with certification of function, as the "somewhat less-risky" secondary market. — [C&EN](https://cen.acs.org/business/instrumentation/happens-old-scientific-instruments/102/i18)
- The eBay risks institutions cite are no recourse for damage or non-function (VT) and the need for independent verification that operating equipment works (UW). — [VT](https://www.procurement.vt.edu/content/dam/procurement_vt_edu/procedures/how/eBay.pdf); [UW](https://www.wisconsin.edu/uw-policies/uw-system-administrative-policies/purchasing-card-compliance-policy/sys-351-appendix-b-commonly-questioned-items/)
- On eBay, "no returns" listings are common. Buyer feedback mentions damage in transit, for example on heavy centrifuge rotors. — [eBay Labnet Prism R listing](https://www.ebay.ca/itm/236932257579); [eBay Hettich Rotanta listing](https://www.ebay.com.au/itm/198512499062)

### Inferences
- A clear position exists between "as-is eBay" (deep discount, high risk, hard for procurement) and full-service refurbishers (30-50% off, with warranty). Tested equipment with a 30-90 day warranty, sold with a business invoice, directly addresses the objections universities have written down.

### Gaps
- I found no buyer survey that measures the discount at which labs switch to used equipment, or how much they value a warranty.
