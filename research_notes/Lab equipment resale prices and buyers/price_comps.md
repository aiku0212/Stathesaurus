# Used Lab Equipment: Auction vs. Resale Price Comps (Boston/Cambridge sourcing, as of Oct 2026)

Method note: research ran about 18 search/fetch calls on 2026-10-14. Direct fetches of ssllc.com, equipnet.com, surplusmarketplace.com and procurement.ofa.ncsu.edu all failed with DNS errors (ENOTFOUND), so every figure below comes from search-result snippets. **Almost no realized auction hammer prices for individual items turned up.** The resale-side figures are mostly dealer or eBay asking prices, not completed sales, unless a line says "sold". Treat everything in the "hammer" column as an assumption to validate. Do not read it as data. Research only: every eBay bid or purchase must be placed by a human. eBay's 2026 user agreement bans AI buying agents and unauthorized scraping.

## Key Question 1: Price-comparison table and net margin per item

### Takeaway
Used resale prices can be documented for about 10 common, parcel-shippable items (microcentrifuges, T100 PCR machines, NanoDrop 2000, Eppendorf/Rainin pipettes, Vortex-Genie 2, Olympus objectives). The best documented spreads are on mid-value, small instruments ($500–$3,000 resale). Realized auction hammer prices for single items are almost entirely missing from public search, so margins can only be modeled under assumed hammer prices.

**Fees**
- In the UK, eBay's Business, Office & Industrial final value fee rose from 11.9% to 12.5% on 12 Feb 2026. — [eBay UK seller centre](https://www.ebay.co.uk/sellercentre/news/2026-january/rate-card-change)

### Inferences
- **Margin model (assumptions, not data).** Net = resale − (resale × 13.6% + $0.40) − outbound shipping (unless the buyer pays it) − testing/packing (~$15) − hammer × 1.18 (assumed 18% buyer's premium). eBay charges its fee on the total including shipping, so shipping the buyer pays still costs about 13.6% of the shipping amount. The hammer prices are placeholders I picked at roughly 20–35% of the low dealer asking price. Validate them against a few real closed auctions before bidding.

- A positive margin depends almost entirely on getting a low hammer price. If the hammer reaches about 50% of resale, then fees (~14%), the 18% premium and shipping push most items to around break-even. Bundle lots (like the $4,469 Surplus Marketplace lot) may be where low per-unit costs actually happen, but they need more capital and pickup logistics.
- Items worth under about $75 resale (vortexers, basic stir plates, heat blocks) barely clear fees and shipping. They only make sense as add-ons bought inside larger lots.

## Key Question 2: Best vs. poor item categories

### Takeaway
The evidence favors small, high-value-per-pound items that can be checked by powering on: microscope objectives, pipette sets, microcentrifuges, PCR machines and NanoDrops. Low-value bench items and refrigerated or heavy units are weaker. Cold-chain consumables and hazardous items should be avoided.

### Cited Findings
- A UPlanSApo 40x objective sold for EUR 990, while a lower-grade Plan N 40x sold for $100. Objective value varies roughly 10x by optical grade, so model identification matters. — [eBay.de](https://www.ebay.de/itm/116671627683)
- Shipping is a big cost on refrigerated centrifuges: one eBay 5424R was listed at $600 with $200 USPS shipping. — [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/196884757370)
- The Vortex-Genie 2 sells for about $40–$63, with roughly $27–$30 shipping on top. Shipping costs about as much as half the item price. — [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/277165306287)
- NanoDrop prices vary widely depending on whether a computer and software are included ($6,900 with laptop/software at New Life Scientific vs. $2,500–$3,500 at SSLLC). — [New Life Scientific](https://newlifescientific.com/collections/all?page=51); [SSLLC](https://ssllc.com/product/thermo-nanodrop-2000-uv-vis-spectrophotometer-a199058)
- Auction equipment usually comes without warranties, support or any guarantee of reliability. — [Excedr auction guide](https://www.excedr.com/blog/laboratory-equipment-auctions)
- Dealers like American Laboratory Trading charge a premium for "tested & refurbished" units with a 90-day warranty. — [ALT Research plus](https://americanlaboratorytrading.com/lab-equipment-products/eppendorf-research-plus-single-channel-pipette-0-5-10ul-15284/)

### Inferences
- **Best bets:** (1) Microscope objectives: tiny, cheap to ship, and checkable visually and under a scope. Hammer prices are likely low when they come bundled with whole microscopes. (2) Branded pipette sets (Eppendorf/Rainin), sold as-is with disclosure, or with a cheap gravimetric check on a balance. (3) Non-refrigerated microcentrifuges (5424, Legend Micro 21). (4) Thermal cyclers: a power-on self-test plus one run. (5) NanoDrop 2000, but only with a working PC/software, and it uses a big share of the capital.
- **Poor bets:** refrigerated centrifuges (heavy, $200 freight), -80 freezers, incubators, biosafety cabinets, floor centrifuges (freight only). Anything needing a field-service install or calibration (qPCR optical calibration, analytical balances needing certification). Radioactive, biohazard-contaminated or decontamination-required items. Antibodies, enzymes and kits (cold chain, expiry, and eBay restrictions on some biologics). Low-value bench items under $75.
- Export controls: high-end confocal/laser or some analytical instruments can trigger EAR checks. Restricting eBay listings to US-only shipping avoids this.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on eBay policy for selling reagents/antibodies or on export-control flags for specific lab instruments was gathered.
- There is no sourced evidence on how often auction-bought pipettes or cyclers turn out defective.

## Key Question 3: Speed of sale and a realistic first month on ~$1,000–2,000

### Takeaway
No reliable sell-through or days-to-sell data for lab equipment on eBay or LabX was found. The first-month plan below is an inference built on the price comps and on 2026 auction logistics in Massachusetts.

### Cited Findings
- No Surplus Solutions "Genetown" auction scheduled for 2026 was found. Genetown 131 (Nov 3–5, 2025) was held in Woonsocket, RI, and earlier Genetown sales also used a Woonsocket location. SSLLC's 2026 homepage listed Empress Tx (650+ lots, Aug 10–12, 2026) and Novelstar Pharma (Aug 17–19, 2026). — [SSLLC auctions](https://www.ssllc.com/auctions); [Genetown 65](https://ssllc.com/auction/genetown-65-online-lab-auction)
- Heritage Global Partners (HGP/ALT) lists sales at 9 Technology Drive, Westborough, MA (Nov 2–Dec 11, 2026, dates unclear on the page) and Framingham, MA (Sep 23–24, 2026, pharma process equipment, completed). — [HGP](https://www.hgpauction.com/); [HGP completed](https://www.hgpauction.com/completed-auctions/)
- Federal Equipment is running a "Major Lab Assets & BioTech Equipment – Complete Facility Closure" auction (location not confirmed). — [Fedequip](https://auctions.fedequip.com/major-lab-assets-biotech-equipment-complete-facility-closure)
- University channels: Harvard's Labs Reuse List and the BUMC Equipment Exchange are internal redistribution programs for affiliates, not public sales. MIT.nano ran free "freecycle" days for MIT community members. — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/newsplus/new-life-for-lab-equipment-reuse-list-launches/); [BUMC Equipment Exchange](https://www.bumc.bu.edu/camed/research-impact/research-resources/bumc-equipment-exchange); [MIT.nano](https://mitnano.mit.edu/events/mitnano-building-39-freecycle-event-nov-9-13-27)
- Ginkgo Bioworks notes that buying directly from closing companies is often the best deal, since equipment is usually young and working, and the one or two remaining staff handle sales. — [Ginkgo](https://www.ginkgobioworks.com/?p=267)
- An r/Flipping thread (Mar 2026) shows a newcomer who won a mixed lab lot (centrifuges, molecular testing machines, imaging gear) and was asking whether any resale market exists. That is anecdotal and gives no profit data. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1rleq7w/snapshots/2026-03-06T03%3A05%3A02.06356Z)

### Inferences
- **Realistic month 1 (~$1,500 deployed):**
  - Week 1: register on BidSpotter, HiBid, EquipNet, SSLLC and HGP. Watch 1–2 MA/RI sales and record 20+ closing prices. BU's own exchange is internal only, but a BU student might still ask labs about informal sales.
  - Weeks 2–3: win 3–6 lots totaling $600–$1,200 including premium. Target pipette lots, objectives, 1–2 microcentrifuges or T100s. Budget for pickup: rideshare or Zipcar for 10–40 lb items. Woonsocket, RI is about an hour away.
  - Weeks 3–4: test, photograph and list on eBay. Expect revenue only from early sales, perhaps $500–$1,500 gross in month 1. Most capital stays tied up in inventory.
  - All bids are placed by a human.
- At the modeled ~1.0–1.5x net-on-cost, $1,500 deployed might produce $1,000–2,000 net profit once all of it sells. With no velocity data, the time to sell everything is unknown.
- **Biggest risk:** paying close to resale value at auction. Specialist dealers bid on these same items.

### Gaps
- Sell-through rates and days-to-sell on eBay/LabX for lab equipment: no sourced evidence found.
- Buyer's premiums for specific 2026 auctions (SSLLC, EquipNet, HGP) were not confirmed. The only sourced premium is 13% on Surplus Marketplace.
- No 2025–2026 news coverage of specific Boston/Cambridge biotech closures feeding these auctions was found in this pass.
