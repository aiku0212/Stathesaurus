# Hammer prices, not buyers, decide lab-flip profits

Used small lab instruments resell at well-documented prices: about **$500–1,250 for an Eppendorf 5424 microcentrifuge, $750–1,595 for a Bio-Rad T100 PCR machine, $2,500–6,900 for a NanoDrop 2000, about $340 for an Eppendorf pipette set (sold), and up to EUR 990 for a single Olympus 40x objective (sold)**. The auction side of the spread is almost undocumented. No itemized hammer prices from SSLLC, EquipNet, Heritage/Federal or BidSpotter could be retrieved, so every margin in this report is modeled, not observed. If you win items at 20–35% of resale, the math nets roughly **$150–850 per item, about 1.0–1.5x your all-in cost**, after a 13–19% buyer's premium, eBay's ~13.6% fee and shipping. Margins thin to $75–140 an item at a hammer of 50% of resale, and you break even at roughly 60–70%. So the first two weeks belong to collecting 20–30 real closing prices, not to bidding. Sourcing is open to an individual. You register on each auctioneer's platform, pay within 2–5 business days, take no purchase orders, and pick up from places like Woonsocket, RI. Buyers are reachable two ways: eBay, whose official seller APIs your agents may use but whose terms have banned AI buying agents and AI scraping since February 20, 2026, and direct invoices through university supplier portals. A real buyer market exists: eBay lab stores have thousands of sales, and universities have written procurement rules for buying used equipment. But the evidence that *underfunded academic labs specifically* are turning to used gear because of the 2025–26 funding cuts comes only from vendors, and the same cuts may simply defer purchases. Your best wedge is being a tested, invoiced, returnable seller that a lab's procurement office can actually pay. Being cheaper is not enough.

Prices vary 2–5x for the same model depending on the seller's tier. The auction side is a near-blank. The best data point is a Surplus Marketplace bundle with two Eppendorf centrifuges, Eppendorf and Bio-Rad cyclers and more. ### The modeled net is $150–850 per item, if you buy at a quarter to a third of resale

The model uses eBay's ~13.6% final value fee plus $0.40 per order, which applies to most categories up to $7,500 ([taxomate](https://taxomate.com/blog/ebay-seller-fees)). It assumes an 18% buyer's premium, in the middle of the documented **15% (SSLLC, 2015), 18% (BidSpotter medical) and 19% (Heritage/Federal)** range ([BidSpotter](https://www.bidspotter.com/en-us/auction-catalogues/bscsur/catalogue-id-bscsur10002); [BidSpotter medical](https://www.bidspotter.com/en-us/for-sale/laboratory-and-pharmaceutical-equipment/medical-equipment); [Federal Equipment](https://fedequipblog.fedequip.com/lab-biopharma-equipment-auction/)). It also allows for outbound shipping and about $15 of testing and packing. The hammer figures are placeholders set at 20–35% of the low dealer ask. They are assumptions, not data.

The sensitivity matters more than the point estimates. Solving the same formula for break-even, a 5424 stops making money at a hammer of about **$340 (62% of resale)**, a T100 at about $495 (66%), and a NanoDrop at about $1,415 (71%). At a hammer of 50% of resale, the 5424 nets about $75 and the T100 about $140, roughly 0.2–0.3x on cost. That return can't absorb one dead unit or one return. If you haven't registered for a Massachusetts resale certificate, add 6.25% sales tax on top of hammer plus premium ([BidSpotter SSLLC invoice](https://www.bidspotter.com/en-us/auction-catalogues/bscsur/catalogue-id-bscsur10002)). The agent's max-bid rule should therefore be `(sold comp × 0.864 − 0.40 − shipping − test/pack − target profit − pickup cost) ÷ (1 + premium) ÷ 1.0625 if no ST-4`. A $100 minimum target profit per item builds in a cushion for the defect rate, which is undocumented. Items under about $75 resale, such as vortexers, stir plates and heat blocks, only make sense as free riders inside a lot you bought for something else.

The unknowns that most affect this model are sell-through speed and defect rate. No source gave days-to-sale for lab equipment on eBay or LabX. With $2,000 of capital, speed determines whether you turn inventory once a month or once a quarter. Earning $500–1,000 a month at the modeled $150–300 per item means clearing about **3–6 good flips a month**. That pace is plausible but unproven.

### Small, high-value-per-pound items win; heavy and refrigerated items lose

Microscope objectives are the standout. Value swings tenfold by optical grade: the UPlanSApo sold for EUR 990, while a Plan N sold for $100 ([eBay.de](https://www.ebay.de/itm/116671627683)). That spread rewards anyone who can read a model engraving, and objectives cost little to ship. Branded pipette sets, non-refrigerated microcentrifuges and thermal cyclers come next, since all can be checked with a power-on test plus one run. The NanoDrop has the biggest absolute margin, but it eats half your capital, and its value depends on the computer and software that come with it ($6,900 with laptop versus $2,500–3,500 without) ([New Life Scientific](https://newlifescientific.com/collections/all?page=51)).

Avoid refrigerated centrifuges ($200 parcel shipping on a $600 unit), -80 freezers, incubators, biosafety cabinets, floor-standing units, anything needing field calibration, anything that might hold a radioactive source, and all reagents. Lots naming Ni-63 or electron-capture detectors are a hard stop, because those can go only to a licensed recipient ([NRC](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1505/ML15054A335.pdf)). eBay bans prescription medical devices outright ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/prohibited-restricted-items/medicine-medical-devices-healthcare-products-policy?id=4322)).

## Sourcing auctions take registration, fast payment and a pickup plan

Each sourcing channel lets an individual in, on terms built for dealers. On SSLLC, you create an account on its own platform and the auctioneer approves each registration under sale-specific terms ([SSLLC](https://ssllc.com/about-us/blog/SSLLC-Auctions)). Its closure sales have demanded **full payment within two business days, buyer-arranged removal, and no purchase orders** ([SSLLC Passage Bio](https://ssllc.com/auction/State-Of-The-Art-Biotech-Equipment-Formerly-of-Passage-Bio)). Its Genetown inventory sits at 2010 Diamond Hill Road, Woonsocket, RI, and a logistics desk handles shipping ([SSLLC Genetown 120](https://ssllc.com/auction/Genetown-120-Lab-Equipment-From-Leading-Biotech-Firms)). EquipNet, based in Canton, MA, requires you to register under the exact legal name on your W-9. It prefers EFT and holds check payments four business days ([EquipNet FAQ](https://www.equipnet.com/faqs/)). Its October 2026 calendar lists closure sales for Novartis, Bayer and Evotec ([EquipNet](https://www.equipnet.com/auctions/)). Heritage Global Partners lists Massachusetts sales in Westborough and Framingham ([HGP](https://www.hgpauction.com/)), plus biotech wind-downs such as Cytokine Therapy, Biomea Fusion and Bolt Projects ([HGP biotech](https://www.hgpauction.com/auction-category/biotech-pharmaceutical-medical/)). Federal Equipment is running a complete-facility-closure lab auction ([Fedequip](https://auctions.fedequip.com/major-lab-assets-biotech-equipment-complete-facility-closure)). BidSpotter carried **462 Massachusetts lab and pharma lots** when indexed ([BidSpotter](https://www.bidspotter.com/en-us/for-sale/laboratory-and-pharmaceutical-equipment)), and HiBid has a Massachusetts landing page ([HiBid MA](https://massachusetts.hibid.com/)). GovDeals terms vary by agency, typically payment in 5 business days and removal in 10, and defaulting bidders can be barred ([City of Bryan](https://www.cityofbryan.net/?p=4379)).

Local Boston supply has one more angle. Ginkgo Bioworks notes that buying directly from a closing company is often the best deal, because the equipment is young and the one or two remaining staff handle sales ([Ginkgo](https://www.ginkgobioworks.com/?p=267)). As a BU student, don't count on BU surplus. BU offers surplus capital equipment to other departments first, and an outside sale needs written approval from a dean, chair or unit head, on an as-is basis with a bill of sale ([BU procedure](https://www.bu.edu/research/forms-policies/sale-of-moveable-capital-equipment-procedure-for-the/)). The BUMC Equipment Exchange is open only to BU's research community ([BUMC](https://www.bumc.bu.edu/camed/research-impact/research-resources/bumc-equipment-exchange)). Harvard's Labs Reuse List and MIT.nano's freecycle days are internal too ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/newsplus/new-life-for-lab-equipment-reuse-list-launches/); [MIT.nano](https://mitnano.mit.edu/events/mitnano-building-39-freecycle-event-nov-9-13-27)).

To buy tax-free for resale, register as a vendor through MassTaxConnect and hand auctioneers Form ST-4 ([Mass.gov ST-4](https://www.mass.gov/doc/form-st-4-sales-tax-resale-certificate/download)). Nobody has verified that every auctioneer honors it. Supply is not the constraint: Q2 2026 Massachusetts biotech layoffs hit a two-year low ([NBC Boston/BBJ](https://www.nbcboston.com/boston-business-journal/mass-biotech-layoffs-drop-to-lowest-level-in-over-2-years/3974136/)), yet the October calendars above are full. Abundant supply cuts both ways, since it also keeps dealers and other flippers in every sale.

The agents' legitimate job on the sourcing side is to read the alert emails that SSLLC, EquipNet, Federal Equipment and BidSpotter send to your inbox. From those emails they extract make, model and accessories, flag hazards, compute a max bid and remind you before close and removal deadlines. They should not scrape auction sites, and they should never bid. Monitoring is already commoditized, through Apify scrapers and Municibid's own ChatGPT app ([Apify](https://apify.com/crawloop/labx-scraper); [Municibid](https://info.municibid.com/municibid-chatgpt-app)), so automation gives you no edge on its own. Your edge is knowing which engraving makes an objective worth $990 instead of $100.

## Buyers come through eBay's APIs or a university supplier portal

eBay is the fastest channel to buyers, and its rules now draw a sharp line through your automation plan. A User Agreement update effective **February 20, 2026** bans "buy-for-me agents, LLM-driven bots" and automated flows that place orders without human review. It also extends the anti-scraping clause to AI and LLM bots ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-bans-ai-agents-updates-arbitration-user-agreement-feb-2026/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/22/ebay_updates_legalese_to_ban/)). Seller-side automation through official APIs stays available. Agents can create and revise listings with the Sell Inventory API and sync orders and tracking with the Fulfillment API. Higher call limits and restricted APIs require eBay's free Application Growth Check ([eBay Developers](https://developer.ebay.com/api-docs/static/gs_request-an-application-growth.html)). Sold-price data is the catch. The Marketplace Insights API is limited to approved partners ([eBay MI API](https://developer.ebay.com/api-docs/buy/marketplace-insights/overview.html)), so you pull sold comps yourself in Terapeak inside Seller Hub. The agent can then build a local comp database from those pulls and from your own sales.

The other channels are smaller. DOTmed has a free tier of 30 listings, and its Buy Now sales carry no DOTmed commission ([DOTmed](https://www.dotmed.com/users/my/newupgrades.html?lang=en)). LabX doesn't handle sales itself and has no listing API that could be found, so you post there by hand ([Excedr](https://www.excedr.com/blog/buying-lab-equipment-through-labx-read-this-first)). Facebook Marketplace prohibits medical devices and hazardous materials ([Vendoo](https://blog.vendoo.co/reseller-guide-to-facebook-marketplace-prohibited-items)), but local pickup-only sales of bulky benchtop items still work there and on Craigslist. Your exit valve for slow inventory is consignment: EquipNet takes a **15% seller's premium on exclusive listings and 20% on non-exclusive** ([EquipNet](https://www.equipnet.com/investment-recovery-solutions/individual-asset-sales/)). Selling outright to dealers is faster and pays less ([Lab Manager](https://www.labmanager.com/a-guide-to-selling-used-lab-equipment-2022)).

### Direct invoices turn procurement friction into your advantage

Universities' own rulebooks describe the opening. Virginia Tech "highly discourages" eBay because there is no recourse for dead items and eBay doesn't accept purchase orders. It recommends asking the seller to sell directly to the university instead ([VT](https://www.procurement.vt.edu/content/dam/procurement_vt_edu/procedures/how/eBay.pdf)). Wisconsin's system expects independent verification that operating equipment works ([UW](https://www.wisconsin.edu/uw-policies/uw-system-administrative-policies/purchasing-card-compliance-policy/sys-351-appendix-b-commonly-questioned-items/)), and Purdue requires a call to Procurement before any used-equipment eBay purchase ([Purdue](https://apps.it.purdue.edu/sites/BusinessOffice/Faq/Item/1010)). Harvard bars its PCard from capitalized equipment of $5,000 or more ([Harvard PCard](https://policies.fad.harvard.edu/resource/pcardataglancepdf)). BU requires competitive bids only above $25K and leaves price reasonableness to the department below that ([BU ENG](https://www.bu.edu/eng/files/2022/10/ME_Competitive-Bidding-.pdf)). One r/labrats commenter says their university card can't be used on eBay at all, so they buy personally and get reimbursed ([r/labrats](https://cal1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/labrats/comments/1jrg1wr/lab_equipment_on_ebay)).

The infrastructure to get paid as an individual already exists. Harvard's Supplier Portal lets US individuals self-register, generates a pre-filled W-9, and pays individuals by Zelle ([Harvard FAS](https://adminops.fas.harvard.edu/files/fasadministrativeoperations/files/harvard_supplier_portal_us_individual_self_guided_registration_0_0.pdf)). PaymentWorks, used by many universities, has a sole-proprietor path ([Penn State](https://procurement.prod.fbweb.psu.edu/research-participant-registration-guide)). Exempt buyers such as BU, MIT and Tufts hand vendors Form ST-5. You must keep it on file and charge 6.25% to anyone who doesn't provide one ([BU ST-5](https://www.bu.edu/cfo/files/2024/09/ST-5-Boston-University-Partially-Complete-copy-version-9-6-24.pdf); [MIT ST-5](https://hq.csail.mit.edu/sites/default/files/documents/ST-5%20MIT%20Sales%20Tax%20Exempt%20Form%202024_OpenAI_0.pdf)). eBay collects that tax for you, but on a direct invoice it falls on you.

The trust package that addresses the written objections has six parts: an invoice under a DBA, a signed decontamination statement, a test report with photos or video and the serial number, a 30-day dead-on-arrival return policy, acceptance of PO numbers, and pricing under $5,000. Decontamination certificates are standard across the resale chain ([Lab Manager](https://www.labmanager.com/consignment-can-maximize-returns-on-pre-owned-lab-equipment-23761)). The other natural direct buyers are seed-stage startups. LabCentral already supplies residents with common equipment worth more than $7M ([LabCentral](https://www.labcentral.org/case-studies/satellite-bio-case-study)), so pitch them backup or specialized items, not basics. One caution on terms: offering net-30 to universities ties up capital you don't have, so ask for card or prompt payment until you're past about $5,000 in capital.

## Buyer demand is real, but the underfunded-lab story is unproven

The best evidence that buyers exist comes from what people actually do. A single eBay lab store, reuzeit's Molecular Biology section, shows **8.8K items sold**, and akbuyshop's Healthcare/Lab/Dental store shows 3.0K ([reuzeit](https://www.ebay.com/str/reuzeit/Molecular-Biology-/_i.html); [akbuyshop](https://www.ebay.com/str/akbuyshop/Healthcare-Lab-Dental/_i.html)). Technology Surplus Depot says its inventory comes from public university and government surplus auctions, which is your exact model, already operating at scale ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/str/techsurplusdepot/Lab-Equipment/_i.html?store_cat=5)). On r/labrats, one researcher calls eBay "the only way I buy equipment" ([r/labrats](https://cal1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/labrats/comments/1jrg1wr/lab_equipment_on_ebay)). Universities wouldn't write detailed used-equipment procurement rules (Virginia Tech, Purdue, LSU, Harvard) if their labs didn't buy used gear ([LSU](https://lsu.edu/administration/ofa/procurement/a-z-policies-procedures/usedequipment.php)). Dealers run dedicated startup programs ([New Life Scientific](https://newlifescientific.com/pages/startup)). Seeding Labs, based in Boston, could equip only about a quarter of applicant university departments in 2015–16, and recipients pay fees toward shipping ([UGA](https://site.caes.uga.edu/ftfpeanutlab/?p=1768); [CST](https://blog.cellsignal.com/sowing-seeds-of-research-success-in-developing-countries)). That ratio is dated and reflects subsidized demand.

The size of the market is contested by an order of magnitude. Paid reports put "refurbished laboratory equipment" at **$25–28B globally**, about $7B of it in the US ([GII](https://m.giikorea.co.kr/report/go1737386-refurbished-laboratory-equipment.html); [ResearchAndMarkets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6190898/refurbished-laboratory-equipment-market-report)). These reports contradict each other and publish no methods. SDI analyst Michael Tice puts true used-instrument sales at **about 2–3% of lab equipment sales, roughly $2B a year** ([C&EN](https://cen.acs.org/business/instrumentation/happens-old-scientific-instruments/102/i18)). Trust the smaller number. It is still vastly larger than anything a $2,000 operation can supply.

The funding stress is documented. NIH terminated **2,291 grants** between February and August 2025, rescinding $2.45B ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13037894/)). FY2026 NIH funding was enacted at about $47.5B, rejecting the proposed 40% cut ([CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R43341)), but disbursements reportedly lagged early in 2026 ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/nih-fy2026-appropriations-enacted-what-it-means-for-grantees)). In a June 2025 Instrument Business Outlook survey of unknown sample size, **71% of labs reported capital budget cuts of more than 10%, and 70% had reduced spending on devices under $50K** ([IBO](https://instrumentbusinessoutlook.com/nih-disruptions-and-lab-tool-companies-stocks-react/)). Lab Manager reports used-equipment inquiries up 23% and ties the rise to tariffs and tight budgets ([Lab Manager](https://www.labmanager.com/how-tariffs-and-tight-budgets-are-driving-demand-for-refurbished-lab-equipment-34007)), though its data source wasn't visible.

The honest reading is two-sided. The IBO number describes cuts in exactly your price band, under $50K. A lab that loses its capital budget may make do with a 20-year-old instrument, like the researcher NPR profiled ([NPR](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/npr/2025/02/24/nx-s1-5307157/medical-research-labs-brace-for-possible-funding-cuts-that-could-disrupt-their-work/)), rather than buy a cheap used one. No independent survey or news story shows labs switching to used gear; only vendors claim it ([LQA](https://www.lqa.com/resources/navigating-evolving-tariff-policies-and-supply-chain-uncertainty-with-refurbished-lab-equipment/)). Underfunded academic labs are a plausible customer, not a proven one. Your first 60 days should test that directly: count how many of your sales go to .edu buyers or through supplier portals.

## A 60-day launch plan with a kill test at each gate

The budget below follows from the margin model rather than from any source. Of the $2,000, put about **$1,200 into inventory across two buying rounds**, $65 into a Boston business certificate ([Boston.gov](https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2025/03/2025-New-Business-Certificate-Form_0.pdf)), about $150 into testing supplies and packing (a cheap balance for pipette checks, bubble wrap, boxes), and about $250 into pickup and shipping. Hold roughly $335 in reserve for a return or a dead unit. The division of labor follows platform rules and the lessons of Anthropic's Project Vend ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)). Agents read, extract, compute, draft and remind. You bid, pay, pick up, test, sign, send and decide.

Register on SSLLC, EquipNet, HGP/Federal, BidSpotter, HiBid and GovDeals under your W-9 legal name. Subscribe to every auctioneer's email alerts. | Set up an inventory and comps spreadsheet. Parse incoming alert emails into a lot list with make, model, accessories and hazard flags. Draft the trust-package templates: invoice, decon statement, test report, 30-day return policy. | All registrations approved, and no legal blocker from BU or the licensing unit. |
| 8–21 | Watch 2–3 sales close without bidding. Record 20–30 closing prices on target models. Pull Terapeak sold comps on the same models. Attend one in-person preview if a sale is local. | Compute the hammer-to-resale ratio for each model. Produce a ranked "buy list" with a max bid per model, using the formula above. Draft eBay listing templates per model. | Median closing price at or below **40% of sold comps** on at least three target models. If closes run above 55%, stop: the edge isn't there. |
| 22–35 | Round one: win 3–6 lots totaling $500–700 all-in. Focus on objectives, pipette sets, 1–2 microcentrifuges or T100s. Power-on test, run one cycle or spin, gravimetric-check the pipettes, photograph everything. | Draft listings with specs and test results, and post them through the Sell Inventory API after your approval. Set price bands. Track inventory age, and remind you of removal deadlines. | Fewer than 1 in 5 items dead on arrival. All items listed within 7 days of pickup. |

| 51–60 | Round two: reinvest proceeds plus about $500 of reserve, weighting purchases toward whatever sold fastest. Consign or dump anything listed 30+ days. Run a full P&L. | Report net per item, days-to-sale, return rate, and the channel mix. Recalibrate max bids using your actual sold prices. | Kill or continue: realized net of **$100 or more per item on average**, median days-to-sale under 30, and a positive cash balance. If not, wind down through consignment. |

Three disciplines matter more than any single step. First, never let a bid exceed the computed max, because specialist dealers bid on the same items and paying near resale is the main way this business loses money. Second, treat the agent as untrusted on anything that moves money or speaks for you. It drafts and you send, and it never holds payment credentials. Third, keep the direct-to-lab experiment running even if eBay alone is profitable. The procurement-friendly position is the only part of this model that dealers and the thousands of existing eBay lab sellers don't already occupy.

## Conclusion

This business turns out to be less an arbitrage problem than a measurement problem. Resale prices are public and stable enough to price against. The unknown that decides everything is what a power-on-tested T100 or a UPlanSApo objective actually hammers for at a Woonsocket or Canton sale in late 2026, and no public dataset answers it. Your first two weeks of watching and recording therefore matter more than any automation you build. The agents' highest-value job is turning those observations into disciplined max bids. Monitoring auctions is already a commodity.

Demand points the same way. The buyers exist, but the "underfunded academic lab" thesis is a hypothesis you can test cheaply, not a fact you can bank on. Budget cuts might push labs toward used equipment, or they might freeze purchases outright. A seller who can be paid through a supplier portal, with a decon statement and a return policy, is positioned to win under either outcome. By day 60 your sales channel log will tell you which one is happening, and that data point is worth more than the first month's profit.
