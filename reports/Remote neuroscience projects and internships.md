# Build a home neuro portfolio, then apply now

You can do real neuroscience from home on a student budget. A **Muse 2 headband (~$250) plus the Mind Monitor app (~$15–18)** records raw EEG good enough to replicate published P300 and alpha findings. Free open datasets (OpenNeuro, DANDI, Allen, HCP) support serious analysis-only projects, and free tools (jsPsych, PsychoPy) cover online behavioral experiments. Paid lab and internship work is a different story: **no fully remote undergraduate internship turned up at J&J or any other company searched**. The J&J programs that fit best are on-site or hybrid in New Jersey and Pennsylvania, and they close soon. The **GMAF (Global Medical Affairs) Summer 2027** internship includes a Neuroscience track and closes in Horsham around **October 21, 2026**. The **2027 GD (Global Development) Summer Intern** program covers quantitative and statistical sciences and closes around **November 9, 2026**. J&J's only Summer 2027 undergraduate sales internship found is in **Vision (Jacksonville, FL, on-site)**. **No pharma or medtech sales role comes with an at-home lab.** Sales interns do product training and ride-alongs. Full-time reps handle regulated drug samples or carry surgical "trunk stock" in their cars, and none of that is experimentation. The strongest strategy is to run home projects as a portfolio, apply to J&J this month, and use BU UROP as the funded route to remote analysis work in a lab.

## A $265 headband can replicate published EEG findings

At this budget, the Muse 2 is the best value. It costs $249.99 ([Choose Muse](https://choosemuse.com/muse-2/)). Mind Monitor exports raw microvolt EEG, band powers, and motion data to CSV, and an EEGLAB plugin reads those files directly ([Mind Monitor](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id988527143); [import_muse](https://github.com/amisepa/import_muse)). The device has published validation behind it. **Krigolson et al. captured the N200, P300, and reward positivity with a Muse and a single laptop, and released open code** ([Frontiers in Neuroscience, 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5344886/)). Cannard, Wahbeh, and Delorme compared Muse against a 64-channel BioSemi system and found it usable for power spectra, individual alpha frequency, and frontal alpha asymmetry ([bioRxiv, 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.02.466989.full.pdf)). With four electrodes and no hardware event markers, the Muse is weak on scalp coverage and timing precision. For ERP and spectral replications, though, it is enough.

If you want more than EEG, the **Backyard Brains Human SpikerBox ($299.99)** records EMG, ECG, EEG, and EOG. Its documentation includes P300, muscle-fatigue, and cold-pressor stress experiments, and its free software exports data ([Backyard Brains](https://backyardbrains.com/products/human-spikerbox)). The **BITalino kit (€189)** adds EDA ([PLUX](https://www.pluxbiosignals.com/products/bitalino-revolution-board-kit-ble-bt)). The research-grade open-hardware step up is OpenBCI: the Ganglion is $499–$625 and the Cyton is $999–$1,249, because shop pages list conflicting prices ([OpenBCI](https://shop.openbci.com/collections/bioamplifiers)). Skip Emotiv. Its free tier can't export data, Emotiv itself calls that tier "insufficient for research purposes," and the export license costs **$1,068 per year** ([Emotiv](https://www.emotiv.com/knowledge-base/pro-lite-capabilities); [licensing](https://www.emotiv.com/pages/pro-licensing-table-v1)). Two hard limits apply. Don't do DIY brain stimulation: consumer tDCS devices skip FDA review, and reported harms include burns and seizures ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2017/01/07/507133313/students-zap-their-brains-for-a-boost-for-better-or-worse)). Keep animal work to invertebrate teaching preps such as the cockroach-leg SpikerBox, which falls outside the federal Animal Welfare Act and PHS Policy ([USF IACUC](https://www.usf.edu/research-innovation/research-integrity-compliance/ric-programs/iacuc/statutes-regulations.aspx)).

| Budget | Hardware | Experiments it supports |
|---|---|---|
| ~$265 | Muse 2 + Mind Monitor | Alpha blocking, individual alpha frequency, frontal asymmetry, oddball P300/N200 |
| ~$300 | Human SpikerBox or BITalino | EMG fatigue, ECG/HRV under stress, EOG, basic P300 |
| $500–$1,250 | OpenBCI Ganglion / Cyton | Multichannel ERP, motor-imagery BCI |
| $0 | Open datasets + jsPsych/PsychoPy | Reanalysis, reliability studies, online behavioral tasks |

A statistics background is the real advantage here, because consumer-EEG validation papers rarely test reliability. Useful projects include a **test-retest reliability study of individual alpha frequency or P300 amplitude across days**, a Bayesian effect-size estimate from a few sessions, or a comparison of artifact-rejection pipelines on Muse data. Most engineering-heavy BCI teams can't produce that kind of evidence, and you can.

## Free datasets and $400 online studies cover projects that need no lab

The highest-return, lowest-risk projects use data that already exists. OpenNeuro hosted more than 600 BIDS-formatted EEG, MEG, and MRI datasets from more than 20,000 participants as of its last published count ([Markiewicz et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8550750/)). DANDI holds electrophysiology in NWB format ([INCF](https://neuroinformatics.incf.org/node/357)). The AllenSDK downloads mouse Neuropixels and imaging experiments filtered by brain area ([AllenSDK](https://allensdk.readthedocs.io/en/latest/index.html)). HCP Open Access data only requires registration, but HCP warns that its data are not fully de-identified and that your institution's IRB rules still apply ([HCP terms](https://humanconnectome.org/study/hcp-young-adult/data-use-terms)). UK Biobank offers a student tier at **£500 + VAT**. It requires a named supervisor and takes about 15 weeks to release data, which makes it a thesis-scale commitment ([UK Biobank](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/use-our-data/fees/financial-support/)). Good projects for your profile include a pre-registered multiverse reanalysis of an OpenNeuro oddball dataset, a psychometric reliability study of HCP behavioral measures, or an Allen tuning-curve notebook. Post each one as a GitHub repo plus a preprint. You could also submit to IMPULSE, an undergraduate neuroscience journal with rolling review ([WFU](https://neuroandsociety.wfu.edu/wfu-impulse-review-team/)).

For new behavioral data, building the experiment is free and paying participants is the main cost. jsPsych hosts free on Cognition.run or a self-run JATOS server ([Sussex guide](https://users.sussex.ac.uk/mel29/online_experiments/hosting.html)). Gorilla is free to build and charges per participant ([Gorilla](https://support.gorilla.sc/support/pricing-faq)). Prolific requires at least **$8/hr, recommends $12/hr, and adds a 33.3% academic fee** ([rewards](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/article/9cd998); [pricing](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445239-what-is-your-pricing)). That puts a 100-person, 15-minute study at about **$400**. It is not yet confirmed whether BU holds a Pavlovia or Gorilla site license. Ask Psychological & Brain Sciences before paying.

## Publishing anything requires the BU IRB, even self-experiments

The ethics line is clearer than most students expect. Under the Common Rule, IRB review applies when work is both "research" (aimed at generalizable knowledge) and involves human subjects. Class demos that stay in class are generally exempt from review. **Results you present or publish outside class are not** ([Ohio University](https://www.ohio.edu/research/compliance/human-subjects/classprojects); [Boise State](https://boisestate.edu/research-compliance/?p=2841)). Johns Hopkins, Northwestern, and Boston Children's require prior IRB review even when the investigator experiments on themselves, and investigators should never self-certify an exemption ([Johns Hopkins](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/institutional-review-board/guidelines-policies/guidelines/self-experimentation); [WCG](https://www.wcgclinical.com/insights/irb-exemption/)). In practice, recording yourself to learn needs nothing. Recording roommates for a write-up, or running a Prolific study headed for a poster, needs a BU faculty sponsor and a BU IRB submission. Get a written "not human subjects research" determination even for public-dataset reanalyses you plan to publish.

Two local rules shape what is possible at home. **Cambridge's recombinant-DNA ordinance covers individuals, and Boston requires a public-health permit for rDNA work**, so even a GFP kit at your apartment is off the table ([Cambridge ordinance](https://www.cambridgepublichealth.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Recombinant-DNA-Ordinance-Cambridge-MA.pdf); [BPHC](https://content.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2021/03/BPHC%20Revised%20Guidelines_Biological%20Laboratory%20Regulations_10.03.2019.pdf)). The compliant wet-lab alternative is BosLab, a volunteer-run BSL-1 community lab in Somerville's Boynton Yards. Membership runs $50–$60 per month, and sponsored memberships are available ([Cambridge Day](https://www.cambridgeday.com/2025/09/14/somervilles-community-biology-lab-makes-science-accessible-and-affordable/); [BosLab](https://www.boslab.org/participate)). Neural-data privacy is now a live legal area. Colorado, California, Montana, and Connecticut (effective July 1, 2026) classify neural data as sensitive, and Massachusetts has a pending neural-data bill, H.103 ([Neurorights Foundation](https://www.neurorightsfoundation.org/advocacy/united-states); [malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H103)). These laws mostly target companies above data-volume thresholds rather than student studies. Still, your consent forms should disclose whether a vendor app uploads participants' EEG to the cloud, and knowing the landscape is a credible talking point with neurotech employers.

## No sales job ships you a lab, and remote research mostly means volunteering

The answer to the at-home lab question is a firm **no**. AbbVie's undergraduate sales internship is product training followed by field work where interns are "always accompanied by a District Manager or Sales Rep" ([AbbVie](https://careers.abbvie.com/job/2027-pharmaceutical-sales-intern-undergraduate-in-mettawa-il-jid-31277)). Boehringer Ingelheim interns schedule appointments and help with lunch programs and sample delivery ([BI listing](https://blackcareernetwork.com/job/pharmaceutical-sales-intern-ridgewood-nj)). Pharma reps who handle drug samples work under FDA/PDMA chain-of-custody rules that carry personal criminal liability ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/Files/Publications/2020/06/FDA-Issues-Guidance-on-Prescription-Drug-Sample-Di.pdf?sc_lang=en); [PharmaVoice](https://www.pharmavoice.com/news/1253/614761/)). Medtech reps carry trunk stock and shuttle loaner kits between hospitals ([Move Medical](https://www.movemedical.com/medical-device-trunk-stock-value/); [Medtronic Spine posting](https://www.theladders.com/job/sales-representative-ii-spine-medtronic-nashville-tn_88177654)). That is inventory management, not experimentation. The science-heavy customer-facing role, the Medical Science Liaison, generally requires a PharmD, PhD, or MD ([4 Corner Resources](https://www.4cornerresources.com/career-guides/medical-science-liaison/)). If you want both sales and hands-on neuroscience, combine a sales or sales-operations internship with your own home EEG projects. No single job offers both.

Remote research roles for current undergraduates are rare and mostly unpaid. The best local option is the **MGH Martinos Center's Takahashi Lab, which takes fully remote, unpaid undergraduate volunteers** to extract data from brain images ([Martinos EMI Lab](https://emilab.martinos.org/?p=467)). Paid remote roles generally require a finished degree. Kennedy Krieger's part-time remote neuroscience RA (statistical analysis of behavioral, electrophysiological, and imaging data, about $37k–$60k/yr) requires a science bachelor's ([Kennedy Krieger](https://jobs-kennedykrieger.icims.com/jobs/22882/research-assistant---neuroscience-research/job)), and remote clinical research coordinator roles typically add a year of experience ([UTSW](https://jobs.utsouthwestern.edu/job/20285815/clinical-research-coordinator-i-psychiatry/)). AI-training contracts pay **$40–$80/hr** but ask for advanced neuroscience expertise or a master's or PhD ([OpenTrain](https://www.opentrain.ai/jobs/neuroscience-ai-training-expert--cmrufskqk001e04jfz2glchcn/); [Prolific via Remowork](https://remowork.life/jobs/6a55a5e6ad9313340d891217/neuroscience-ai-training-us)).

BU's own system is the realistic way to get paid. **UROP pays up to $750 per semester (5 hrs/week), up to $1,500 with a faculty matching grant, or $7,000 for a full-time summer** ([BU UROP](https://www.bu.edu/urop/get-involved/funding-eligibility/)). The next deadlines are **December 11, 2026** for Spring 2027 and **March 5, 2027** for Summer 2027, and each requires a mentor recommendation submitted the same day ([UROP deadlines](https://www.bu.edu/urop/wp-json/wp/v2/pages/10655)). No BU page advertises remote undergraduate research, so you'll need to negotiate remote work yourself. Email Psychological & Brain Sciences, Neuroscience, or Center for Systems Neuroscience PIs who work with large EEG, behavioral, or imaging datasets, and offer remote data cleaning and statistical analysis ([BU Psych labs](https://www.bu.edu/psych/research); [BU CSN](https://www.bu.edu/csn/research)). A faculty mentor also solves the IRB-sponsor problem from the earlier section. For a structured remote program, Neuromatch Academy runs a three-week online computational neuroscience course each July, applications close in mid-March, and graduates can apply for paid TA roles ([Neuromatch](https://neuromatch.io/computational-neuroscience-course/)). Free BR41N.IO BCI hackathons pay $400–$1,000 prizes per track ([g.tec](https://www.gtec.at/hackathon/ieee-smc-2026/)).

## Conclusion

Neither home projects nor internships is enough on its own, but they work well together. In industry, the remote and hybrid options open to undergraduates are mostly analytics and desk roles, and the neuroscience-adjacent internships are on-site in New Jersey and Pennsylvania. At home, you can produce what those employers and labs want to see: a pre-registered, statistically careful EEG reliability study or dataset reanalysis with clean, standards-based code. The most valuable step this week is not buying hardware. It is submitting the GMAF application before October 21 and lining up a BU faculty mentor before the December 11 UROP deadline, because that one relationship provides the funding, the IRB sponsorship, and a remote-analysis role all at once.

Finally, drop the hope of a sales job with an at-home lab. The idea runs into how pharma and medtech sales actually work: reps who handle product are tightly regulated, and nobody experiments with it. If neuroscience sales is the long-term goal, the path is a sales or sales-operations internship now, then a post-graduation development program such as the SLDP, with your own EEG work as proof that you understand the science better than most reps.
